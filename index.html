The story

We built NeuroEase because to-do lists were breaking our brains.

Standard productivity tools assume a brain that can plan, start, and sustain tasks on command. For neurodivergent people, that assumption is the problem. NeuroEase is a different approach: tools that adapt to how your brain actually works, backed by research, built with the community.

Understanding neurodivergence

Neurodivergence is variation, not deficit.

An estimated 15-20% of people process information, attention, and emotion differently from the neurotypical baseline. This section explains what that means in plain terms.

Neurodivergence describes natural variations in how the brain processes information, manages attention, regulates emotion, and interacts with the world. It includes ADHD, autism, dyslexia, dyspraxia, Tourette syndrome, and related cognitive differences. These are not disorders to be cured — they are differences in cognitive wiring that come with both strengths and challenges.

The term was coined by sociologist Judy Singer in the late 1990s and has grown into a movement that rejects the pathologising of natural human variation. The neurodiversity framework does not deny that these conditions can cause real difficulty — it insists that the difficulty comes from a mismatch between the person and their environment, not from something inherently broken inside the person.

Source: Singer, J. (1998). NeuroDiversity: The Birth of an Idea.

ADHD is not just trouble paying attention. It is a difference in how the brain manages executive functions — the set of cognitive processes that let us plan, start tasks, hold information in working memory, regulate emotions, and shift between activities. When these functions are impaired, it is called executive dysfunction.

In practice, executive dysfunction looks like:

  • Knowing you need to start a task but being unable to make your body begin
  • Losing track of time even for things you care about (time blindness)
  • Feeling overwhelmed by multi-step instructions or cluttered environments
  • Emotional flooding after perceived criticism or rejection (RSD)

None of these are laziness. They are neurological barriers that no amount of "just try harder" can fix. Effective strategies work with the brain's actual wiring, not against it.

Source: Cleveland Clinic (2023). Executive Dysfunction. Barkley, R.A. (2015). ADHD: A Handbook for Diagnosis and Treatment.

Autism affects how a person experiences sensory input, processes social information, and manages routines and change. Many autistic people have heightened or reduced sensitivity to light, sound, touch, smell, or internal body signals (interoception). Environments that seem normal to others can be painfully overwhelming.

Key autistic experiences that productivity tools rarely account for:

  • Sensory overload from bright lights, noise, or visual clutter that makes focus impossible
  • Difficulty identifying or naming emotions (alexithymia), which standard mood trackers unknowingly demand
  • Monotropism — intense, sustained focus on one interest at a time, which rigid timers interrupt rather than support
  • Need for explicit, literal communication — implied expectations or vague instructions create anxiety, not motivation
Source: Kofler et al. (2024). Executive function in ADHD and ASD. Nature Reviews Psychology, 3, 701-719.

NeuroEase takes the neurodiversity stance: neurological variation is a normal and valuable part of human diversity. The goal is not to make neurodivergent people function like neurotypical people. The goal is to build tools, environments, and expectations that accommodate different cognitive styles — so that everyone can contribute, thrive, and work without fighting their own brain.

This stance is backed by a growing body of research in sustainable HRM, organisational psychology, and inclusive design. Companies that embrace neurodiversity consistently outperform their peers on innovation, retention, and productivity metrics.

Source: Rollnik-Sadowska & Grabinska (2024). Managing Neurodiversity in Workplaces. Sustainability, 16(15), 6594. McKinsey (2023). Diversity Matters Even More.
About symbols and colors. The rainbow infinity loop is the neurodiversity-affirming symbol created by the autistic community in 2005 — it represents neurodivergence broadly without implying anything needs fixing. Gold (Au, the chemical symbol for gold) specifically represents autism acceptance. Orange represents ADHD awareness. Green represents mental health awareness. This project uses none of these as default UI colors, respecting that identity markers should be chosen by the user, not applied by the product.

Evidence-based strategies

Practical techniques that work with your brain, not against it.

Each strategy below is grounded in peer-reviewed research or established clinical practice. None of them require willpower — they work by reducing friction.

1

Break tasks into micro-steps

Instead of "clean the kitchen," start with "put one dish in the sink." Absurdly small steps remove the activation barrier that prevents initiation.

NeuroEase: The micro-step engine auto-breaks any task into its smallest honest version.
2

Use a visual timer

Time blindness makes abstract time unreliable. A visual countdown externalises time perception so your brain doesn't have to track it internally.

NeuroEase: Every task shows a progress-based visual timer, not a clock.
3

Body double for task initiation

Having someone present — physically or virtually — measurably improves focus and follow-through. The mechanism seems to be social presence reducing the emotional weight of starting.

NeuroEase: Optional ambient body-doubling mode with syncable co-working sessions.
4

Schedule by energy, not by time

Instead of assigning every hour of the day, assign tasks to energy states: high-focus work to morning if that is when you focus best, low-demand tasks to afternoon slumps.

NeuroEase: Energy-aware scheduling across social, sensory, cognitive, and physical domains.
5

Build quiet into your environment

Sensory overload destroys focus. Noise-cancelling headphones, dimmer lighting, visual decluttering, and defined quiet spaces reduce cognitive load for everyone, not just autistic users.

NeuroEase: Low-stimulation theme is the default. Focus mode hides everything except the current task.
6

Write it down and put it where you can see it

Working memory is one of the first executive functions to run out of capacity. Externalising task lists, instructions, and deadlines onto a visible medium frees cognitive resources for actual work.

NeuroEase: The task board is always one tap away, never buried in menus.
Not every strategy works for every brain. Try what fits. Skip what does not.

The same principles that make a tool work for a neurodivergent brain — clear structure, low clutter, predictable navigation, adjustable pace, written instructions, quiet defaults — measurably improve outcomes for everyone. This is not a trade-off. Designing for cognitive diversity does not take anything away from neurotypical users. It raises the baseline for everyone, in the same way that curb cuts (designed for wheelchair users) benefit parents with strollers, delivery workers, and anyone on a rolling bag. When you build for the edges, the centre works better too.

What makes NeuroEase different

Not just another mood tracker.

Most wellbeing apps assume you can notice, name, and report an emotion. For many neurodivergent people, that assumption is the barrier. Here is what we do instead.

Body-first check-ins

Before asking "how do you feel," ask what the body is doing: tight chest, racing thoughts, heavy limbs. Emotion labels get suggested from body data, not demanded up front.

Energy/capacity tracking over mood scores

Log available capacity across separate domains (social, sensory, cognitive, physical) instead of collapsing everything into one number. This makes adaptive scheduling actually adaptive.

Masking-cost tracker

Optionally log when you are masking and how draining it was. Over weeks, see which environments and interactions cost the most — turning an invisible burden into something visible and plannable.

Monotropism-aware focus protection

Detect and protect hyperfocus once it starts. Fewer interruptions, gentler transition warnings, and an explicit "you are in flow, hold notifications" prompt instead of cutting focus at an arbitrary 25 minutes.

RSD spiral check

A lightweight sequence for moments of rejection-sensitive dysphoria: separate "what happened" from "what my brain is telling me it means." No mainstream mood app addresses this by name.

No-guilt skip with data

When a task gets skipped, log why as data, not failure. Over time the pattern surfaces what actually blocks you — without shame.

Shareable state card

A one-tap summary: "today I have low capacity for talking, high capacity for focused work." Show it to a manager or partner without disclosing or justifying your neurotype.

Flexible streaks with grace days

A grace-day bank prevents total reset on missed days. Rigid streaks increase shame and dropout for ADHD/burnout-prone users — grace days keep momentum without punishment.
